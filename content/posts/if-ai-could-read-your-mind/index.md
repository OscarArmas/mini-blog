For years, the bottleneck was compute, data, infrastructure. Lately, I’ve been wondering if, for a lot of tasks, it’s becoming us. We keep getting more capable models, agents, skills, plugins and tools that gather more context. And I still find myself struggling to explain what I actually want.

Try writing down an idea, then explaining it out loud for a few minutes. Sometimes you hear yourself say something you hadn’t even thought to write. You give it more detail, more context, and still look at the result and think: “That’s not quite what I had in mind.” How many times have we done this while vibe coding? You start with a picture in your head and somehow end up building something else.

That’s why I find BCIs, brain-computer interfaces, so interesting. They can’t read everything we think today. But imagine being able to pass an idea directly from your brain to an AI. The interface you’ve been picturing, a game, maybe even a dream. You could finally make it look exactly the way it did in your head.

I’d love to try that. But then I wonder: would I actually know what to build?

Because plenty of times, the problem is that I haven’t figured out what I want yet. I have a rough idea, with a lot of missing details. I figure some of them out by building, trying things, getting them wrong. My imagination is limited by what I know and what I’ve experienced, too. Something could turn out exactly as I pictured it and still be something nobody needs.

As a programmer, this is where it gets uncomfortable for me. I watch AI get better at tasks I spent years learning, and I start asking myself where I can still contribute. So we move toward building products, starting companies, becoming “orchestrators.” But what happens when the product you’re working on becomes a skill or a built-in tool a few months later? I wonder what that would do to my motivation to keep creating.

I keep coming back to context. The things we know, what we’ve lived through, the problems we’ve dealt with firsthand. There’s value in that. Sometimes you know a problem matters because you’ve had to live with it, long before you could explain it properly to a model.

But if these systems could someday access much more of our thoughts and experiences, what would happen to that contribution? We’d be giving them our confusion, contradictions, and biases along with everything useful. Would that help us understand what we want, or would we still be stuck with the same questions, just able to communicate them faster?

I don’t know how far any of this will go. What stays with me is the possibility that we start as creators using these tools and gradually become sources of context for them. I want AI to understand what I mean. I want to spend less time trying to get an idea out of my head. But I’m still trying to figure out what I’d want to do once that part became easy.
